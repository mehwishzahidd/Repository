# Track 4 — Competitive Programming (Codeforces & friends)

> Solving algorithmic puzzles **fast and correctly, under a clock**. It's a sport, it's
> genuinely fun, and it makes you dramatically sharper at coding interviews.

**Prerequisite:** Core DSA (Track 2). Competitive programming *is* applied DSA under time
pressure — there's no point starting before you know the basic data structures. A fast
language (**C++**, Track 3) helps a lot at higher levels, though you can start in Python.

---

## Why it matters for your goals
- **Interview sharpness:** The single best way to get fast and accurate at coding problems.
- **Quant firms love it:** Many quant trading firms recruit directly from competitive
  programmers (Codeforces rating is a real signal to them).
- **It's measurable & motivating:** Your rating goes up as you improve. Instant feedback.

---

## How competitive programming works
- **Codeforces** runs frequent "Rounds" (contests). You solve 5–8 problems in ~2 hours.
- You get a **rating** (like chess Elo). Everyone starts around 0; divisions:
  - **Div 4 / Div 3** — beginner-friendly rounds (start here!)
  - **Div 2** — intermediate
  - **Div 1** — advanced
- Other great judges: **AtCoder** (clean problems, Beginner Contests are excellent),
  **CSES Problem Set** (a curated topic-by-topic ladder), **USACO** (structured tiers).

---

## Syllabus / topic ladder (in rough order of difficulty)

### Beginner (Codeforces rating ~800–1200)
1. **Implementation & simulation** — just translate the problem carefully into code
2. **Math basics** — parity, divisibility, GCD/LCM, modular arithmetic
3. **Greedy** — sort-and-pick patterns
4. **Two pointers & prefix sums**
5. **Basic binary search** (including "binary search on the answer")

### Intermediate (~1300–1700)
6. **Graphs** — BFS/DFS, connected components, shortest paths (Dijkstra)
7. **Dynamic programming** — classic patterns (knapsack, LIS, grid DP)
8. **Number theory** — sieve of Eratosthenes, primes, modular inverse
9. **Combinatorics** — counting, nCr

### Advanced (~1800+)
10. **Segment trees / Fenwick (BIT)** — range queries
11. **Advanced graphs** — DSU/union-find, MST, topological DP
12. **Advanced DP** — bitmask DP, DP on trees, digit DP
13. **String algorithms** — hashing, KMP, Z-algorithm

---

## How to practice
- Do the **beginner Codeforces rounds** (Div 3/4) and **virtual contests** (re-run old
  contests under timed conditions).
- Climb the **CSES Problem Set** topic by topic — it maps almost exactly to the ladder above.
- After each contest, **upsolve**: solve the problems you couldn't finish in time. This is
  where the real learning happens.

## Free resources
- **Codeforces.com** — the main arena; make an account and do a Div 4 round
- **CSES Problem Set** (cses.fi/problemset) — the best structured topic ladder
- **USACO Guide** (usaco.guide) — superb free, organized curriculum with C++ & Python
- **AtCoder** (atcoder.jp) — Beginner Contests (ABC) are great for newer competitors
