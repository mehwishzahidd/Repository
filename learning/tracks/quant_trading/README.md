# Track 7 — Quant Trading

> Using math, statistics, and code to trade financial markets profitably. One of the most
> intellectually demanding and **competitive** fields in tech/finance — a fantastic
> long-term goal that rewards everything else you're learning here.

**Prerequisites (be honest with yourself here):** This is an **advanced, specialized**
end-goal. It sits on top of:
- **Strong math** — especially probability & statistics ([Track 6](../math_for_quant/))
- **Solid programming** — Python and often C++ ([Tracks 1 & 3](../cpp/))
- **DSA** — quant interviews test algorithms hard ([Track 2](../dsa/))

Don't start *here*. Build those first; this track is what they're building *toward*.

---

## What quants actually do (the main roles)
- **Quant Researcher (QR)** — invent trading strategies/signals using math, stats & ML.
  Heavy math; Python/R.
- **Quant Developer (QD)** — build the fast, reliable systems that run the strategies.
  Heavy engineering; C++/Python. **DSA-heavy interviews.**
- **Quant Trader (QT)** — manage risk and execute; fast mental math & probability under pressure.

---

## The interview (what to actually prepare)
Quant interviews are famous and specific. They test:
1. **Probability & expected-value brainteasers** — e.g. "expected number of coin flips to
   get two heads in a row?" (← Track 6 prepares you for these)
2. **Mental math** — fast arithmetic, estimation (Fermi problems)
3. **Market-making games** — they quote you a price and you learn to manage a position
4. **Statistics** — distributions, hypothesis testing, regression
5. **DSA / coding** — often as hard as or harder than big-tech (← Track 2)
6. **"Do you think well?"** — they probe how you reason under uncertainty

## Syllabus (once your foundations are ready)

### Part A — The toolkit
1. **Probability for trading** — EV, variance, conditional probability (from Track 6)
2. **Statistics for trading** — distributions, correlation, regression, time series
3. **Mental math & estimation** — drills for speed
4. **Market microstructure** — how exchanges, orders, bid/ask, and market-making work

### Part B — Strategy & modeling
5. **Financial instruments** — stocks, options, futures (the basics of what's traded)
6. **Backtesting** — testing a strategy on historical data (Python: `pandas`, `numpy`)
7. **Signals & alpha** — building and evaluating predictive features
8. **Risk management** — position sizing, the Kelly criterion, drawdowns
9. **Intro to ML for trading** — regression → simple predictive models (advanced)

### Part C — Interview prep
10. **Brainteaser drills** — the famous probability puzzles
11. **Market-making mock games**
12. **Quant coding problems** — DSA with a quant flavor
